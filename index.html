FIRST: WHERE TO GO?

Holiday flights: where, how much, when?

Booking flights around major holidays means answering three questions in order: where should we go, how much will the ticket cost, and when should we buy it?

We tracked one-way fares searched April 16 – October 5, 2022 for Memorial Day, July 4, and Labor Day — plus a non-holiday baseline. Start with the fun question: where to go?

1. Where to go?

Not everyone has a destination yet. This map shades each state by the median fare to fly there from our origin — darker costs more. Hover for prices, click states to build a shortlist.

2. How much will the ticket cost?


Destination picked? Now check what kind of ticket. Around July 4, nonstop flights came out cheaper than connecting ones — backwards from what most people assume. Never assume the connection saves money; price both before you start watching the calendar.

3. Daily checks
are mostly noise.

Each square is one day of fare checks, 60 days out on the left, color-coded by whether matched fares rose, fell, or barely moved. On a typical day, ~10% rise 10%+, ~10% fall 10%+, and ~80% barely move. Press Next to group them.

Grouped by outcome, most days are flat grey. Daily checking gives you nothing to act on — the signal is hiding somewhere else. Press Next to find it.

There it is: big jumps land on a few specific days, right after the 21-, 14-, and 7-day advance-purchase cutoffs. Fares move in steps, not slopes.

So stop watching for a gradual climb you can react to — it never comes. Scroll down to see the clearest staircase of all: Labor Day.

4. The fare sits flat, then jumps overnight


Labor Day is the clearest staircase: the median lowest fare slid from $290 at 60 days to $258 at 14 days, jumped to $284 the next morning, then climbed to $371 the day before departure — 44% above the floor. A traveler watching for a gradual climb never sees it coming.

5. The cheapest moment moves by route

Each row is a route, each column a 10-day window. Red = fares rose, blue = fell. The late columns are red everywhere — but the low point lands at different times per route.

6. Waiting is a bet you usually lose

Every flight, every day, lands in one band: it jumped 10%+, fell 10%+, or sat steady. Watch the red band swell at the cutoffs.

Sketch SS5 · Near the cutoffs at least 1 in 4 flights rise 10%+, while on most days fewer than 1 in 6 fall that far. You can't predict the cheapest day — but you can predict the days it gets more expensive.

So when should you book?

Pick the destination on the map, price the nonstop, then buy before the 21-day cutoff. Once inside it, jump risk climbs from ~10% to 25%+, the median fare climbs with it ($258 → $371 on Labor Day past 14 days), and the seats drain away. One rule beats fare-watching — set your buy-now band and stop hunting the low point.

Data Sources and Methods

Data

  • Expedia flight prices (primary): one-way fares searched Apr 16 – Oct 5, 2022. Attributes: searchDate, flightDate, startingAirport, destinationAirport, airline, legId, baseFare, totalFare, isNonStop, travelDuration, seatsRemaining, cabin; derived route, days-before-flight, holiday window (May 26–31, Jun 30 – Jul 5, Sep 1–6; baseline Jun 9–14, Aug 11–16).
  • U.S. BTS DB1C/OD40: origin-destination flows and fares by purchase window. DOT Table 6: city-pair fares, low-cost vs largest-carrier shares.
  • BTS On-Time Performance: noted as out of scope for the delays question (Q6).

Methods

  • Medians of the lowest fare per route-day; matched legId tracking across search dates for ±10% moves; advance-purchase cutoff comparison (21/14/7 days).
  • State map and team-note values are layout samples until the team pastes real medians (see the override hook in js/charts.js).
  • Out of scope with current data: cookie-clearing effects, points-vs-cash, driving-vs-nearby-airport costs.
