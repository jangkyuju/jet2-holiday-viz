BUY BEFORE 21 DAYS OUT

Is waiting for a cheaper fare real?

Booking flights around major holidays means guessing when to buy. Should you grab a fare two months out, or hold out for a last-minute drop?

We tracked one-way fares searched April 16 – October 5, 2022 for Memorial Day, July 4, and Labor Day — plus a non-holiday baseline. Let's look at the data to see whether hunting the cheapest day actually works.

Daily fare checks
are mostly noise.

Each square is one day of fare checks, 60 days out on the left, color-coded by whether matched fares rose, fell, or barely moved. On a typical day, ~10% rise 10%+, ~10% fall 10%+, and ~80% barely move. Press Next to group them.

Grouped by outcome, most days are flat grey. Daily checking gives you nothing to act on — the signal is hiding somewhere else. Press Next to find it.

There it is: big jumps land on a few specific days, right after the 21-, 14-, and 7-day advance-purchase cutoffs. Fares move in steps, not slopes.

So stop watching for a gradual climb you can react to — it never comes. Scroll down to see the clearest staircase of all: Labor Day.

The fare sits flat, then jumps overnight


Labor Day is the clearest staircase: the median lowest fare slid from $290 at 60 days to $258 at 14 days, jumped to $284 the next morning, then climbed to $371 the day before departure — 44% above the floor. A traveler watching for a gradual climb never sees it coming.

What is the 21-day rule?

Three findings from our data explain it: the cheapest moment moves by route, waiting costs you seats as well as dollars, and the jumps cluster at predictable cutoffs. You can't predict the cheapest day to buy — but you can predict the days it gets more expensive. So stop hunting the low point and just buy before the 21-day cutoff.

    3. Waiting is a bet you usually lose

    Spikes at the cutoffs

    So when should you book?

    Buy before the 21-day cutoff. Once inside it, jump risk climbs from ~10% to 25%+, the median fare climbs with it ($258 → $371 on Labor Day past 14 days), and the seats drain away. One rule beats fare-watching — set your buy-now band and stop hunting the low point.

    Data Sources and Methods

    Data

    • Expedia flight prices (primary): one-way fares searched Apr 16 – Oct 5, 2022. Attributes: searchDate, flightDate, startingAirport, destinationAirport, airline, legId, baseFare, totalFare, isNonStop, travelDuration, seatsRemaining, cabin; derived route, days-before-flight, holiday window (May 26–31, Jun 30 – Jul 5, Sep 1–6; baseline Jun 9–14, Aug 11–16).
    • U.S. BTS DB1C/OD40: origin-destination flows and fares by purchase window. DOT Table 6: city-pair fares, low-cost vs largest-carrier shares.
    • BTS On-Time Performance: noted as out of scope for the delays question (Q6).

    Methods

    • Medians of the lowest fare per route-day; matched legId tracking across search dates for ±10% moves; advance-purchase cutoff comparison (21/14/7 days).
    • Out of scope with current data: cookie-clearing effects, points-vs-cash, driving-vs-nearby-airport costs.
